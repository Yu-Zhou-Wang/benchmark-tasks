All task geometry in `latch.usda` is original procedural CAD generated by
`../build_assets.py`; no manufacturer meshes, photographs, textures or logos
are redistributed. Length units are metres. The model is a generic slotted
chain-door latch, not a dimensionally exact replica of a branded product.

The mechanism was checked against these primary references on 2026-11-16:

- [Yale WS6 fitting instructions](https://static-mpc.assaabloy.com/yalefile/Fetchfile.aspx?dl=1&id=15656): the plate mounts horizontally near the opening edge of the door; the chain's staple mounts on the frame; the end fitting passes through the plate's slot. These establish which parts move with the door and how the end is engaged.
- [Yale security door chain](https://yalehome.co.uk/security-door-chain/): identifies the household use of a chain holding a door partly open.
- [Sugatsune CS-1000](https://www.sugatsune.com/s-s-door-chain-cs-1000/) and its [manufacturer drawing](https://www.sugatsune.com/content/site-assets/201B_PDF/163.pdf): stainless steel construction, 101 mm plate length, 22.5 mm width and 18.5 mm projection provide a size reference. The simulated 105 × 38 mm housing and 22 mm grasp tab are a deliberately accessible bench fabrication, not claimed manufacturer dimensions.

Mechanical inferences from the modeled geometry are tested in simulation:
the head cannot pass the narrow opening; a finite chain limits the door; and
moving the fitting toward the far opening requires extra chain length, obtained
by closing/supporting the door. The plate's dimensions and tolerances are design
choices rather than measurements of a commercial sample.

The plywood panel and frame are a reduced bench fixture; the hardware remains
hand sized. A native torsion spring on the hinge represents a reproducible light
opening load. The ring connections are permanent passive pin joints, with
alternating axes, rather than contact-resolved interlinked rings. These joints
are always present. There is no joint between the fitting and the door.

`scenekit.py` is adapted from the locally validated RoboCurve scene adapter used
by `lock-before-loading` and the peristaltic-cassette development work. It is
vendored here so the task does not import another task or workspace path. Its
callbacks read geometry and restore fixed spring targets at reset; the task
does not alter object state in response to progress.

The straight-line endpoint-distance diagnostic is not actual free slack when
the chain wraps the panel edge. No force-calibrated tension estimate is claimed.
The tests measure the opening limit and physical fitting retention directly.
